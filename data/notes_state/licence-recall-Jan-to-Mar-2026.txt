Prison Licence Recalls: January to March 2026
Publication details
These tables are published as part of the 'Offender Management Statistics Quarterly' publication by the Ministry of Justice.
Link to 'Offender Management Statistics Quarterly' (opens in a new window)
Definitions and measurements
Further details of the terminology used to report prison population statistics can be found in the definitions section for population in the 'Guide to Offender Management Statistics' published alongside these tables.
Geographic coverage
All tables are for England and Wales.
Data sources
The figures in these tables are drawn from the Public Protection Unit Database (PPUD).
Data quality
The figures in these tables have been drawn from administrative IT systems which, as with any large scale recording system, are subject to possible errors with data entry and processing.

Note Description
[note 1] HDC recalls refer to recalls during home detention curfew period.
[note 2] In June 2021, Community Rehabilitation Companies (CRCs) were combined with the National Probation Service (NPS) into one Probation Service (PS). We use "National probation" to refer to both NPS and PS.
[note 3] Regional grouping under the new Probation Service replaces the old regional grouping.
[note 4] There is an emergency process for all indeterminate recalls.
[note 5] Offenders are returned in target time if the end-to-end process takes less than 74 hours for emergency process recalls and less than 144 hours (6 days) for standard process recalls.
[note 6] Probation Trusts were replaced with National Probation Service/Community Rehabilitation Companies before the Offender Rehabilitation Act 2014 introduced recalls for sentences of less than 12 months.
[note 7] From June 2021, classification of offences is based on Home Office's counting rules for recorded crime.
Home Office counting rules for recorded crime -data.gov.uk (opens in a new window)
[note 8] Recall reasons do not sum to the total number of recalls as more than one reason can be recorded against each recall.
[note 9] Offenders serving Indeterminate and Extended Determinate sentences are not eligible for a fixed-term recall. Offenders serving sentences of less than 12-months are eligible to receive a fixed-term recall of 14-days.Offenders serving sentences of 12-months or more are eligible to receive a fixed-term recall of 28-days.
[note 10] An individual may be counted multiple times if recalled repeatedly in a given quarter.
[note 11] Figures for fixed-term and standard determinate recalls before July 2025 are not comparable to figures from July 2025. Previously, some recalls were recorded as "emergency" recalls without reference to whether they were fixed-term or standard recalls, and these were placed in the 'Other' recall length category.
[note 12] From 31 March 2026, 56-day Fixed-Term Recall (Fixed Term - 56 days) replaced the previous 14-day and 28-day fixed-term recalls for eligible offenders. Figures for 56-day Fixed-Term Recall in January to March 2026 include some cases where offenders were recalled before 31 March 2026 but returned to custody after that date, and whose recalls were subsequently converted to 56-day Fixed-Term Recall. As a result of this legislative change, data from 31 March 2026 onwards are not directly comparable with those from earlier reporting periods.
